1. The split

Three series, 7 games, all patch 16.20, all played on 2026-10-31. They are the last three series of Worlds 2026, pinned by series_id in pipeline.TEST_SERIES so the test set does not move when the corpus is rebuilt. A fractional tail inside a patch would be a different set of games every time; a test split that moves is not a test split.

2. What is compared

2 next-move checkpoints and 3 masked ones, one per model class. The directory name states the inference: masking-* completes a draft and answers the role board, nextpick-* ranks the next move. masking-soloqueue is not a class of its own - it is the encoder masking-finetune starts from, kept as the reference that says whether fine-tuning on pro drafts bought anything.

4. Masked completion

Each of a game's ten picks is hidden in turn while the other nine stay visible, so 70 slots per model. The models are asked the question the role board asks.

The masked models cannot express fearless. On the games that carry a fearless context, 85 of 120 masked slots put a champion already picked earlier in the series into their top-5. The next-move models do get unavailable_mask and break fearless zero times. That is a scope limit of the masked task as trained, not a bug in this harness.

5. How much seven games can say

Almost nothing, on its own. At 7 games one game is 14% of the sample, and a 95% interval on a rate observed over seven games is roughly 35% wide. Any two models whose intervals overlap are tied as far as this split can tell, and at this n most of them will. The intervals are printed next to every rate for exactly that reason.

What the split can do is catch a model that is broken or badly fitted - a next-move model at or below the frequency table, a masked model that cannot name the champion at all. It is a smoke test on genuinely unseen games, not a ranking. For the ranking, the within-patch validation split is the number to read: it is 100x larger and every model was selected on it.

The one comparison that survives the sample size is the per-state one, because every model is scored on the identical board. That is what the step tables below are for: not "which model wins" but "here is what each one said at this state, and here is what happened".
