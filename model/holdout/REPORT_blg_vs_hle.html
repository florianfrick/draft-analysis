What this does and does not say

  • The pro-only models beat the kept pipeline here, but not on equal information. Every pro-only policy is ahead of policy-ablF on top-5 and mean rank. The catch: under the date split all 196 patch-16.20 drafts are test rows, so ablF never trained on this patch and has no patch input to compensate; the pro-only models trained on 155 of them and carry a patch embedding. The gap therefore mixes three things — better fit, a patch prior, and strictly more information about 16.20. What it does show is the deployment behaviour: a patch arrives, you have its first 80%, and the model that can use that beats the one that cannot.
  • ban1 separates the arms, as the mechanism predicts. With an empty board the patch-conditioned models reach 58% top-5 while the no-patch arms sit at 17%. n=12 states, so treat it as direction, not magnitude.
  • The masked models are fearless-blind. They never receive unavailable, so on game 2 they happily name champions that BLG and HLE had already used — 56 of 80 slots put a game-1 pick in their top-5. That is a scope limit of the masked task as trained, not a bug in this harness.
  • No value head is usable. Three of the four are flat near 50%; the policy value head swings between 0.01 and 0.99 within a single draft, which is the same failure STATUS.md §3 records at test time.
  • Accuracy is the weakest thing on this page. With two games and 40 states, a one-hit difference is 2.5 points. The per-step tables are the result; the totals are an index.
